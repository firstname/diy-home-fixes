How to Unclog a Slow Draining Sink

Posted on Mon 14 September 2026 in Home Improvement

A sink that drains slowly is telling you the blockage is partial and close to the surface. Skip the bottle of chemical drain cleaner — it rarely reaches the clog, it sits in the trap eating at the pipe, and if it fails you have a basin of caustic water to work around. Start with the trap instead; it takes ten minutes and it is where most sink clogs actually are.

Step 1: Clear the trap

Put a bucket under the U-shaped pipe beneath the sink and unscrew the slip nuts by hand — they are designed to be hand-tight. Have the bucket ready before the last one comes off, because the trap is full of water by design. Tip the contents out and poke a bent wire hanger through the trap itself; hair and soap scum usually come out as a solid plug.

Step 2: Snake the wall arm

If the trap was clear, the blockage is further along, in the pipe running into the wall. A small hand auger — the cheap drum type with a cable — reaches the first metre or so, which is where the clog almost always sits. Feed the cable in gently and rotate; do not force it, because forced cable can scratch older metal pipe.

Step 3: Reassemble and test

Screw the trap back together by hand, then tighten a quarter turn with a wrench. Run the hot tap for a full minute and check the joint with a dry finger — any moisture at all means the slip nut needs another nudge.

What not to do

Do not use chemical drain cleaner repeatedly, and never use one immediately before opening the trap: caustic residue in the pipe ends up on your hands and in the bucket.
