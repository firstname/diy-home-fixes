How to Fix a Door That Won't Close

Posted on Mon 14 September 2026 in Home Improvement

A door that suddenly refuses to latch is almost never a problem with the door itself. Nine times out of ten it is one of three things: loose hinge screws, a swollen frame, or a latch that has drifted out of line with the strike plate. Work through them in that order — it is the cheapest-first sequence, and the first fix resolves most cases.

Step 1: Tighten the hinge screws

Open the door and try lifting it by the handle. If it moves, the hinge screws have worked loose and the door is sagging onto the frame. Tighten every hinge screw, top to bottom. If a screw spins without biting, the hole is stripped — pull it out, glue in a wooden matchstick or a golf tee, snap it flush, and drive the screw back in.

Step 2: Check the strike plate

Close the door slowly and watch where the latch meets the strike plate. If it is catching on the top edge, the plate needs to move down; if on the bottom edge, up. Loosen the plate screws, tap it in the required direction with a hammer and a block of wood, then re-tighten. Filing the plate opening is a last resort — moving the plate is reversible, filing is not.

Step 3: Deal with a swollen frame

If the door binds along the whole latch side and the problem appeared in humid weather, the frame has swollen rather than the door dropping. Do not plane the door immediately. Run a dehumidifier in the room for a couple of days first, since the frame often shrinks back on its own. If it still binds, mark the high spots with a pencil held flat against the frame and plane only those.

What not to do

Resist the urge to plane the door first. It is irreversible, it is the messiest option, and in most cases the door was never too big — it was hanging slightly wrong.
