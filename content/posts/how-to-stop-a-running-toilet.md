A cistern that keeps refilling is almost always the flapper — the rubber seal at the bottom
of the tank that lifts when you flush. It costs very little, it can be swapped without
turning off the house water, and it fixes the majority of running toilets in one go.

### Step 1: Confirm it is the flapper

Take the lid off the cistern and put a few drops of food colouring in the tank. Do not
flush. Wait fifteen minutes — if colour appears in the bowl, water is seeping past the
flapper and that is your leak.

![Food colouring test showing a leaking toilet flapper]({static}/images/how-to-stop-a-running-toilet-step1.jpg)

### Step 2: Replace the flapper

Turn off the stop valve at the wall and flush to drain the tank. Unhook the old flapper
from the two lugs at the base of the overflow tube and lift the chain off the flush lever.
Fit the new one in the reverse order. If the chain has more than about 25 mm of slack it
can catch under the new flapper, so shorten it.

### Step 3: Check the fill level

If a new flapper does not fix it, look for water running down the overflow tube. That means
the fill valve is set too high and is continuously topping up into the overflow. Most fill
valves have an adjustment screw or a clip on the float — lower the waterline to roughly
25 mm below the top of the overflow tube.

### What not to do

Do not reach for the cistern lid adjustment or start bending the float arm before doing the
dye test. It takes fifteen minutes and tells you which of the two parts is actually at
fault, instead of guessing at both.
