A hole up to about the size of a fist does not need a new piece of plasterboard. A
self-adhesive mesh patch and two thin coats of compound will disappear completely under
paint — and unlike the old "stick a sponge in the hole" trick, it will not crack along the
edge a year later.

### Step 1: Cut the hole back to clean edges

Trim away any torn paper and loose crumbs with a utility knife so you are left with a clean
square or circle. Feather the paper edge very slightly; a ragged paper lip is what makes a
patch bulge.

![A mesh patch pressed over a trimmed hole in drywall]({static}/images/how-to-patch-a-small-hole-in-drywall-step1.jpg)

### Step 2: Apply the mesh patch

Peel the backing and centre the self-adhesive mesh over the hole, pressing it firmly onto
sound plasterboard on all sides. The mesh is the reinforcement — without it, compound
bridging a gap will crack.

### Step 3: Two thin coats, not one thick one

Spread joint compound with a 100 mm knife, working from the edges inward, and leave it very
slightly proud of the wall. Let it dry fully, then apply a second, wider coat that feathers
out to nothing at the edges. One thick coat shrinks and cracks as it dries; two thin ones
do not.

### Step 4: Sand and prime

Sand with 120-grit paper wrapped round a flat block — never with your fingertips, which
dig hollows. Prime the patch before painting, or the paint will flash: the patched area
absorbs differently to the surrounding wall and shows as a dull spot.

### What not to do

Do not skip the primer. It is the single most common reason a patch you cannot feel is
still perfectly visible once the paint dries.
